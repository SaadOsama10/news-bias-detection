📰 News Bias Detector

Paste a news text and see whether it reads like opinion (Biased) or reporting (Neutral). Runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is sent anywhere.

What this does — and doesn't — measure. The model was trained on The Guardian’s Israel–Palestine coverage, where “Biased” means opinion-style writing (the Comment is free section) and “Neutral” means news-section reporting. It detects writing style, not political truth or factual accuracy, and it may be unreliable on other topics or outlets.

      How it works

      Your text→lowercase + tokenize→drop stop words→1–2-word terms→TF-IDF (5,000 terms)→Linear SVM→Platt scaling→Biased / Neutral
      • The classifier is a TF-IDF + Linear SVM trained on 3,750 Guardian articles and scored on 1,250 unseen ones: 97.12% test accuracy, ROC-AUC 0.995.
      • Every term has a learned weight; the decision is the sum of weight × TF-IDF value over the words in your text. The highlighted words are the largest terms in that sum.
      • The percentage is a calibrated probability (Platt scaling fitted on held-out training scores), not a raw SVM score.
      • This page re-implements scikit-learn’s preprocessing and decision function in plain JavaScript. On all 1,250 test articles its predictions match the Python model (parity test).
      • Because the labels come from the newspaper section, a confident “Biased” means “reads like a Comment-is-free column”, not “is politically slanted”. Short or off-topic texts are less reliable.
